# Peer Review — True PUCT / full node expansion (issue #21)

## Summary

The branch replaces one-child-per-iteration expansion with AlphaZero-style full expansion: `_mcts_iteration` now descends by PUCT to a childless leaf, scores it once (outcome for terminal, evaluator otherwise), attaches a child per legal ply seeded with its prior, and backpropagates from the leaf itself — so the root is evaluated by iteration 1 and its children get real priors for the first time. `unexplored_plies`, `is_fully_expanded`, `node.policy`, the expansion shuffle and the `_visit_distribution` reconciliation are all gone; `PositionEvaluation.policy` became required and `NullEvaluator` now supplies a uniform policy. Both plan and story are otherwise faithfully implemented, and the implementation plan does include the README/docs check (Step 5).

**Tooling:** `pyright` reports **0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 informations**. `ruff check .` reports **All checks passed**. `pytest` is green (123 passed in ~5s), so no findings are filed from any of the three.

**Verification performed for this review.** The plan's Step 3 blunder-rate table was reproduced exactly (1090 distinct non-terminal positions at ≤4 plies played: 12.3% at 50, 3.6% at 200, 0.3% at 1000 — matching the recorded figures), which is what surfaced finding #4. Step 4's claims were also checked: 200 iterations with the checked-in weights costs ~40 ms per ply (plan says ~30 ms — close enough not to file), and over 153 self-play samples no legal ply received a hard-zero visit share, confirming the "targets sharpened, not degenerate" conclusion. `examples/tictactoe`'s 200,000-iteration null engine is *faster* after the change (9.7s vs 11.6s for the opening ply on `main`), so full expansion carries no throughput regression there.
